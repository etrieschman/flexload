# The V test, 2026-11-11

How much of a result comes from the value function $V$, and how much from the type $(\beta, A)$?

## Setup

- Code at commit `d29d104`.
- Firm loads (the mover), 10% of system load, the four tightest weeks (29, 34, 35, 28).
- ERCOT with `tau_0_ercot` and PJM with `tau_0_pjm`. Level 2 times the largest unit, margin 0.06.
- Exogenous price-taker.
- Base values: training $\psi = 100$, $\beta = 0.5$, energy conserved over the week; inference $\chi = 500$, $\beta = 0.1$, nothing conserved.
- "Welfare gain" is $S^R - S^{B0}$, welfare after the load responds less the baseline. It is not the flexibility value $S^{B1} - S^{B0}$; the planner was not run.
- The script was a one-off and is not in the repo.

## A. The share of each result in hours at the tolerance bound

Welfare is a sum over hours.
In an hour where the bid sits at $\beta\delta_t$, the bid is pinned by $\beta$ and does not depend on $V$.

| market | workload | hours at the bound, of 672 | welfare gain (M\$) | share at the bound | rent change (M\$) | share at the bound | MWh moved | share at the bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ERCOT | training | 56 | 17.95 | 106% | −1,085.7 | 101% | 49,820 | 48% |
| ERCOT | inference | 46 | 6.36 | 100% | −383.9 | 100% | 3,899 | 100% |
| PJM | training | 21 | 0.88 | 135% | −92.1 | 99% | 24,809 | 36% |
| PJM | inference | 13 | 0.10 | 100% | −9.8 | 100% | 1,124 | 100% |

A share above 100% means the other hours lose a little.
For training that is the cost of putting the energy back in ordinary hours.
Inference is at the bound whenever it moves, because its $V$ is linear.

## Findings

1. **Under ERCOT the results do not depend on the shape of $V$.**
   All of the welfare gain and the rent change sit in hours at the bound.
   Halving or doubling $\psi$ moves the training load's welfare gain by under 10% and its rent effect by 1%.
2. **$\beta$ moves the results more than $V$ does.**
   That is expected: $\beta$ is the size of the response.
3. **The rent result is robust for training under both markets.**
   It moves by 2% or less with $\psi$.
4. **For inference the strike is a threshold, and where it sits against each market's top price is the result.**
   ERCOT's top price is about \$4,800, so a strike between 250 and 1,000 changes the gain by 15 to 20%.
   PJM's top price is \$850, so the strike decides everything: at 1,000 the load never answers PJM's scarcity and its welfare gain is negative.
   Report this as a finding about the strike, not as a parameter choice.
5. **PJM's welfare gains are small and move a lot.**
   The training gain of 0.88 M\$ moves by 30 to 60% with $\psi$.
6. **A larger $\beta$ lowers the training load's welfare gain under both markets.**
   A load that can move more leaves the system worse off.
   My reading, not verified: the exogenous price-taker expects baseline prices to hold, and at 10% of the system its own move removes them, so it over-responds.
   This is a reason to run the consistent price-taker before trusting a "tariff loses" number.

## What this does not show

- It uses welfare after the response, not the planner's optimum, so it says nothing about what the tariff loses.
- It covers four weeks. The ordinary weeks, where $V$ has more say, are not in it.
- It holds $A$ fixed. Energy conservation was not varied.
- The curtailable contract and the hard N−1 row are not in it.
