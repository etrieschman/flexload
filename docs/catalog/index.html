Wholesale cost-allocation tracker

Tracker of cost-allocation mechanisms for wholesale electricity consumption, covering conforming and nonconforming loads. Each record separates a charge into three parts: the pool of dollars being recovered, the base that is measured, and the charge that results. Every entry is taken from source text and carries a citation and a quotation.

Notation used in the formulas
k
The layer. Superscript on the base, the charge and the pool, so x^k, Phi^k and C^k always belong to the same record.
e
The entity the market bills - the scheduling entity for almost every layer, the distribution utility for transmission. Not an individual load.
e^{\prime}
Another such entity. Appears only in the denominators that turn a pool into a share.
n
A node - a single point on the transmission network. ERCOT calls these Electrical Buses; node is used here because that is the term the wider literature uses for the same object.
z
A Load Zone - a group of nodes ERCOT prices together. Load settles against the zone rather than the node, so the zone is also the settlement point for load. Used bare, z ranges over the whole zone, which is what prices are averaged across; determinants instead range over the entity's own zones, written Z_e.
\mathcal{N}_{z},\; \mathcal{T}_{t},\; \mathcal{M},\; \mathcal{A},\; \mathcal{V},\; \mathcal{E}
Sets are calligraphic capitals, their elements the matching lowercase letter. In order: the nodes making up zone z; the intervals making up a span, written T_t for the dispatch intervals inside settlement period t and T_{m,y} for the settlement intervals inside month m of year y; the four summer peak months; the ancillary service products; the real-time money flows; and all billed entities. A sum over any of these ranges over the whole system.
\mathcal{N}_{e,z},\; \mathcal{Z}_{e},\; \mathcal{P}
The entity-scoped sets: the nodes inside zone z at which entity e serves load, the zones in which it serves load, and the service periods of a contract it is billed across. Determinants sum over these, never over the system-wide sets - an entity is billed on its own load, not on everything inside a zone it happens to occupy. The distinction carries the content of the energy layer, where the base sums the entity's own nodes while the price multiplying it is averaged over every node in the zone, whoever serves load there.
t,\; s
The settlement period being billed, and a dispatch interval inside it (s in T_t). Settlement period length varies by layer - fifteen minutes, an hour, a month - and each record states which under its measurement window. ERCOT redispatches roughly every five minutes, so several s fall inside one t and prices must be averaged across them.
m,\; y
Calendar month and year. Used only by the annual peak-demand layer.
a
An ancillary service product. ERCOT procures five of them.
\mathrm{AML}_{e,z,t}
Adjusted Metered Load - the quantity nearly every determinant in this inventory is built from, and not the same thing as metered consumption. ERCOT defines it as retail usage adjusted for unaccounted-for energy, transmission losses, distribution losses and DC tie exports, so it is larger than what the meter recorded. Losses are recovered by inflating this quantity rather than by any separate charge, which is why the loss layer is marked absorbed. Written with a node subscript instead of a zone subscript, as AML_{e,n,t}, in the one layer that works nodally.
\mathrm{LRS}_{e,t}
Load Ratio Share - the entity's fraction of total truncated metered load in the same period, x^k_{e,t} divided by the sum of x^k over all entities. This one ratio allocates most pooled costs in ERCOT, which is why so many layers share a determinant: once an entity's consumption share is known, so is its share of nearly every pool. Shares sum to one by construction, because the denominator is the sum of the same truncated quantities that form each numerator. Contemporaneous unless a record says otherwise.
\max(0,\;\cdot)
The truncation ERCOT applies to an entity's metered load in each of its zones before summing them. An entity whose load nets negative in one zone is treated as zero there rather than credited, so shares are built from truncated quantities and not from net load.
x^{k},\; \Phi^{k},\; C^{k}
The base (what is measured), the charge (what is billed) and the pool (the dollars being recovered), for layer k.

How every charge is built

One layer of the bill, for one entity, in one billing period. Everything in the table below is an instance of this.

\Phi^{k}_{e,t} \;=\; \underbrace{\sigma^{k}}_{\text{sign}}\; \underbrace{r^{k}_{t}}_{\text{rate}}\; \underbrace{x^{k}_{e,\,\mathcal{W}^{k}(t)}\!\left(d_{e}\right)}_{\text{base}}

de is the entity's demand path — the only thing it controls. t is the period billed; Wk(t) is the window the base is measured over. For most layers those coincide; where they do not is where the interesting layers live.

The rate is a pool over an estimate of the base

r^{k}_{t} \;=\; \frac{C^{k}_{t}}{\hat{X}^{k}_{t}} \qquad\text{where}\qquad \hat{X}^{k}_{t} \;=\; \text{the total base the rate was built on}

That single choice — which denominator the rate was built on — determines everything about recovery. Collections and the residual both fall straight out of it:

\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\Phi^{k}_{e,t} \;=\; C^{k}_{t}\,\frac{X^{k}_{t}}{\hat{X}^{k}_{t}} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad R^{k}_{t} \;=\; C^{k}_{t}\left(1-\frac{X^{k}_{t}}{\hat{X}^{k}_{t}}\right)

So there is no separate true-up mechanism. A true-up is just a rate whose denominator is revised to the realised base.

The dominant case

Eleven of the fourteen charged ERCOT layers are this one expression, with the residual identically zero.

\Phi^{k}_{e,t} \;=\; \sigma^{k}\,C^{k}_{t}\,\frac{x^{k}_{e,\,\mathcal{W}^{k}(t)}}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}}x^{k}_{e^{\prime},\,\mathcal{W}^{k}(t)}}
